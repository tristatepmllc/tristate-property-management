## The first few hours decide the claim

Everything about restoration is easier if the first response was recorded.

Photographs before anything is moved. Moisture readings before drying begins. A note of what was wet, how far it had spread, and when it was found. None of this slows the work down, and all of it is what an adjuster asks for later.

The alternative is a building that has been correctly and expensively restored, with no evidence of what it was restored from. That is a weaker claim than a smaller loss that was properly documented.

## Drying is measured, not judged

Water damage is mostly invisible. What is on the floor is the small part; what has wicked into drywall, subfloor, insulation and cavities is the part that decides whether there is a mould problem in six months.

So the work is run against readings. Moisture meters on the affected materials, readings taken and logged daily, equipment adjusted as the numbers move, and drying declared complete when the materials reach the dry standard - not when the surface feels right.

This is also the part that protects you afterwards. A logged drying record is the answer to a later question about whether the building was dried properly, and there is no way to reconstruct one retrospectively.

## Where restoration turns into build-out

Once a property is dry and safe, the rest is construction: demolition of what cannot be saved, reinstatement of what can, and finishes back to their original standard or better.

That is the same work as a tenant fit-out or a unit make-ready, which is why they share a page. A make-ready between tenancies is a small build-out on a deadline. A fit-out is a larger one with a lease date attached. Both live or die on sequencing.

## Sequencing is what you are actually buying

On a multi-trade job the wasted time is almost never inside a trade. It is between them.

The floor cannot go in until the paint is done. The paint cannot be done until the drywall is finished. The drywall cannot be closed until the plumbing and electrical are in. With four separate contractors, each handover is a phone call, a scheduling gap and someone waiting for someone else - and every one of those gaps is a day a unit is not let or a space is not trading.

Running the trades in-house under one work order removes most of that. It is the single clearest reason this work costs less and finishes sooner when it is not split between four companies, and it is worth more than any line-item discount.

## What we do not do

We do not negotiate your claim. We produce the documentation, the scope and the costs, and your adjuster does the rest.

We do not perform abatement of asbestos, lead or established mould growth. Those require a licensed remediation contractor, and in older commercial and multifamily stock they are found often enough that it is worth expecting. We identify it, stop, and bring in the right party rather than disturbing it.
