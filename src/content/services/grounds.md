## Two jobs at once

Grounds work is usually bought as presentation - the site should look cared for. That is real, and for retail, dealerships and anywhere leasing space it is directly commercial.

But the exterior is also where a building's liability concentrates. Ice on a walkway, a pothole in the car park, a trip hazard at a kerb, an unlit path, a branch over a footway. Almost every slip and fall claim against a commercial property starts outside the front door.

That means how the work is recorded matters nearly as much as whether it was done. Which is why our winter service records carry timestamps, and why hazards found on a routine visit get reported rather than just noted.

## Winter is a documentation exercise

A snow and ice claim is not argued in February. It is argued the following autumn, when someone asks what was done on a specific morning eight months earlier.

If the answer is a service record showing arrival time, conditions and what was applied, the conversation is short. If the answer is that somebody remembers coming out, it is not.

We record each visit for exactly this reason. It also settles the more everyday argument about whether the site was serviced during a marginal event, when the difference between freezing rain and rain is a judgement call made at four in the morning.

Contract shape is the other decision. Per event tracks reality and suits mild or unpredictable winters. Seasonal fixes the budget and moves the risk of a heavy winter to us. Neither is universally better - it depends on whether an unpredictable line item or a higher predictable one is the bigger problem for you.

## Landscaping on the right interval

The most common false economy in grounds maintenance is stretching the visit interval.

Grass, beds and edges do not degrade linearly. Past a certain point the work needed to recover a site is materially more than the work that would have maintained it, and in between the site looks like nobody is responsible for it. On a leasing or retail property that is a direct commercial cost, not an aesthetic one.

The interval that works is driven by the growing season, so a sensible contract is dense in the middle of the year and light at the ends, rather than an averaged monthly visit that is too little in July and unnecessary in January.

## Car parks and hard surfaces

The car park is the largest single surface most commercial sites own and the one most likely to generate a claim.

Sweeping and litter are presentation. Standing water, potholes, faded markings, damaged kerbs and blocked gullies are risk, and they all worsen quietly. Water sitting on a surface accelerates its failure and freezes in winter, so drainage problems and pothole problems are usually the same problem at different stages.

We report what we find on routine visits rather than waiting to be asked, because the cheapest time to deal with any of it is early.
