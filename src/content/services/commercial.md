## Every building has a backlog

Walk any commercial property with the manager and the list writes itself: the lot light that has been out for a month, the stained ceiling tile, the faded parking lines, the restroom fixture that never quite gets fixed. None of it is urgent on its own. Together, it is what tenants and customers notice.

A maintenance contract works through that backlog on a schedule instead of waiting for each item to become a complaint.

## Outside is the first impression

The parking lot, the landscaping and the front of the building are what people see before they open the door. Sealcoating and striping protect the asphalt as much as they improve the look. Graffiti removed within days rarely comes back; graffiti left for weeks invites more.

## Inspections that lead somewhere

A building inspection is only useful if it ends in a decision. Ours finish with a short list sorted by risk and cost: what has to happen now, what should be budgeted for next year, and what can be watched. Photos are attached to every item so the list can be shared with owners and approved without another site visit.
