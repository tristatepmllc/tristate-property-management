Most emergency HVAC calls we take in August were preventable in April. A rooftop unit rarely
fails without warning — it gets louder, it runs longer, the condensate line backs up, and then
on the first 95-degree afternoon it stops. The cost difference between a scheduled filter and
coil service and an after-hours compressor replacement is not small.

## Quarterly

- Replace filters. In a building with heavy foot traffic or nearby construction, monthly.
- Wash condenser and evaporator coils. Dirty coils are the single most common cause of a unit
  running long and drawing more power than it should.
- Clear and treat the condensate drain line. A blocked line is what puts water through a
  ceiling tile.
- Check refrigerant charge and log it. A charge that drops every quarter means a leak, not a
  top-up.
- Inspect belts, bearings and electrical connections. Tighten what has moved.

## Twice a year

- Test economiser operation. A stuck economiser quietly wastes a great deal of energy.
- Verify thermostat calibration against an independent reading.
- Inspect ductwork for disconnected runs and damaged insulation.

## What to keep a record of

Log the date, the unit, the readings and the technician. When a unit reaches the end of its
life you want a maintenance history to justify replacement rather than another repair — and if
you ever sell or refinance the building, that record is worth having.

## When to stop repairing

A rule of thumb: once annual repair spend on a single unit passes about half the cost of
replacement, and the unit is past two thirds of its expected service life, replacement is
usually the cheaper decision over a five-year horizon. Run the numbers on your own units rather
than trusting the rule.
