The honest answer is that it depends on the building, but the reason two quotes for the same
job differ by a factor of two is usually structural, not competitive. Here is what sits
underneath the number.

## The three pricing models

**Time and materials.** You pay an hourly rate plus parts. Fair for unpredictable work, hard to
budget against. Ask what the minimum call-out charge is and whether travel time is billed.

**Flat rate per job.** A written price before work starts. Better for anything well defined —
a fixture swap, a lighting retrofit, a floor strip and wax.

**Contract or per-square-foot.** Recurring services like janitorial and grounds. Cheaper per
visit, but read what is excluded; many contracts price the routine work low and bill everything
else as an extra.

## Where the variance comes from

- **Subcontracting.** A vendor who subs out plumbing adds a margin on top of the sub's margin.
  In-house crews remove that layer.
- **Minimum charges.** A one-hour job billed at a four-hour minimum is not a cheap job.
- **After-hours multipliers.** Nights and weekends are commonly 1.5x. If your building can only
  be worked on outside business hours, that is your baseline, not an exception.
- **Trip charges per trade.** Five separate vendors means five trip charges. One crew handling a
  punch list in a single visit does not.

## Questions worth asking before you sign

1. Is the minimum call-out charge per visit or per trade?
2. What is excluded from the recurring contract?
3. Are your technicians employees or subcontractors?
4. What is the after-hours rate, and when does after-hours start?
5. Will I get a written quote before work begins on anything above a set threshold?

None of these are unreasonable questions, and how a vendor answers them tells you more than
the headline rate does.
